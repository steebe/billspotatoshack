     Pete — endless-runner game for Bill's Potato Shack

     Context

     prompts/PETE.md asks for a second mini-game, "Pete": a side-scrolling
     endless runner where Potato Pete is pinned on the left and jumps over farm
     hazards while the world scrolls and speeds up. Day/night cycles with
     distance, and the farmland turns arid past a score threshold. It follows
     the same pattern as the existing catch/ game: plain HTML + one JS file +
     shared style.css, no dependencies, and well under the 512kb budget (target
     ~15–20 KB total).

     The mechanics follow the Dino Game genre (jump-only runner, speed ramp,
     day/night). All art is original pixel art, potato/farm themed and in the
     brand palette. No T-rex, cactus, pterodactyl or Chrome assets.

     Files

     - New pete/index.html: copy of catch/index.html structure (header/nav,
       <main class="pete">, HUD, .stage with canvas + .overlay, controls hint,
       footer). Links ../style.css?v=3 and game.js?v=1.
     - New pete/game.js: an IIFE in the style of catch/game.js.
     - Edit style.css: add a .pete section. Reuse .hud, .overlay, .overlay 
       button, .controls-hint, .hint-touch/.hint-desktop as-is. Add .pete-stage
       (wide aspect, e.g. 3:1 at desktop, width: min(100%, 800px),
       image-rendering: pixelated on the canvas). Add new color tokens to :root
       so the game palette has one source of truth: --leaf, --leaf-dark,
       --sky-day, --sky-night, --arid, --arid-dark, --poison.
     - Edit index.html and catch/index.html: add a Pete! nav link next to
       Catch!. Bump the style.css?v= cache-buster to 3 on all pages.

     game.js design

     Reuse from catch/game.js: the CSS-var color loader (getComputedStyle → C),
     the rand helper, the frame() loop with clamped dt, setupCanvas() DPR
     scaling, overlay end()/start(), and HUD caching.

     Pixel look: logical resolution is small (e.g. W=320, H=110) and scaled up
     with imageSmoothingEnabled=false and CSS image-rendering: pixelated.
     Sprites are tiny string-grid bitmaps ('..##..' rows plus a per-sprite
     palette map) drawn with fillRect per pixel and pre-rendered once to
     offscreen canvases per palette (day / night / arid) so each frame is cheap.

     Sprites (frames):
     - Pete: idle/run (1–2 frames of a slight bob) + jump frame (eyes wide,
       little arms up), in yukon colors.
     - Weed small, weed large (taller/wider): static.
     - Herbicide can with a skull/X-bones "poison" icon: static.
     - Beetle: 2 leg frames.
     - Bird: 2 wing frames (up/down).

     World/physics:
     - speed starts ~120 px/s and ramps with score (speed = min(MAX, BASE + 
       score * k)). score = distance / some factor, shown as a 5-digit
       zero-padded counter plus HI.
     - Static obstacles move at speed. Beetles and birds move at speed * 
       1.15–1.3.
     - Spawning: gap is randomized but scaled by speed so a jump is always
       possible. Birds only unlock after score ~300, and low/high birds are
       picked at random. High birds clear Pete's standing head (no action
       needed); low birds sit at jump height so the player must jump over them.
     - Variable jump: on press, vy = -JUMP_V. While the input is held and
       holdTime < MAX_HOLD (~0.18s), apply reduced gravity so a long press
       floats slightly longer at the apex. Releasing early cuts vy (vy = max(vy,
       -CUT_V)) for a short hop. Pete can't jump again until he's grounded.
     - Collision: AABB with per-sprite inset hitboxes (forgiving by a few px).

     Layers / parallax (move separately):
     - Sky (fills, day/night color lerp), sun/moon + stars at night (static or
       very slow).
     - Far hills/crop rows at 0.2×speed, clouds at 0.1×speed.
     - Foreground ground strip + tiled dirt/grass texture at 1×speed. Obstacles
       at 1× or more.

     Day/night: every NIGHT_EVERY = 700 points, toggle the night flag. A nightT
     0→1 tween over ~1s blends the palettes (sky color lerp; sprites swap to a
     pre-rendered night palette once nightT > 0.5).

     Arid farmland: once score >= ARID_AT = 1500, the aridT tween turns
     grass/hill colors toward --arid tones, crops into dry stubble, and adds an
     occasional tumbleweed-ish dust puff in the background (decorative only).

     Game states: ready (overlay "Help Pete run!" + Start), running, over
     (overlay "Pete got squashed" / "Pete got weeded", etc., with score + "Play
     again"). High score is saved in localStorage (wrapped in try/catch).

     Input: Space / ArrowUp / W to jump (hold for higher). Pointer/touch down on
     the stage jumps, and pointer up ends the hold (with a touch fallback like
     Catch). Space/Enter starts from the overlay. Clear the held state on blur.
     Respect prefers-reduced-motion by skipping the screen-shake/flash on
     game-over.

     Verification

     1. python3 -m http.server in the repo root, then open /pete/.
     2. Check: Pete jumps; a tap gives a short hop and a hold gives a higher,
        longer jump; collisions end the run; score climbs and speed visibly
        ramps; beetles/birds close in faster than weeds/cans; high birds pass
        overhead while standing still; low birds need a jump; day→night at 700
        and back at 1400; arid look at 1500; HI score persists across reloads.
     3. Mobile: Chrome devtools device mode, tap to jump, layout fits with no
        horizontal scroll.
     4. Size: wc -c pete/* style.css index.html stays well under 512 KB (expect
        <25 KB).
     5. Nav links work from home, Catch, and Pete.

● Pete is built and playable at /pete/. Run python3 -m http.server in the repo
  and open localhost:8000/pete/.

  I checked it with headless Chrome screenshots of the day, night, arid,
  arid-at-night and mid-jump scenes, plus a phone-width view. I also simulated
  the jump physics. I haven't played a full run with real input, so the
  difficulty tuning is the thing to check by hand.

  What's in it
  - Pete is a pixel potato pinned on the left. He has a running pose and a jump
    pose with wide eyes, an "O" mouth and arms up. He also wears the jump face
    when he gets mashed.
  - Ground hazards that move with the ground: small weeds (sometimes in pairs),
    large weeds, and red herbicide cans with a skull-and-crossbones label.
  - Hazards that close in faster: potato beetles (orange with black stripes)
    with moving legs at 1.2× speed, and crows with flapping wings at 1.25×. High
    crows pass over Pete if he stays on the ground, but jumping into them kills
    him. Low crows have to be jumped.
  - Variable jump: a quick tap gives a hop of about 19px, and holding gives
    about 44px with a slower float at the top. The large weed is the obstacle
    that needs a held jump. A press just before landing still counts.
  - Difficulty: speed starts at 160 px/s and climbs with score up to 400. Large
    weeds start appearing at 150 points, beetles at 250 and crows at 450.
  - Scenery: three layers scroll at different speeds (clouds, hazy hills, crop
    rows), separate from the ground. Night falls every 700 points and day
    returns at the next 700, with stars and a moon. At 1500 points the farm
    dries out for the rest of the run: tan stubble, a dusty sky and tumbleweeds.
  - Pixel look: the game draws at 300×120 and the browser scales it up with
    crisp pixels.
  - Score: the HUD shows the score and the high score (HI), which is saved in
    localStorage. Game-over messages depend on what hit Pete, e.g. "Mashed! Pete
    got sprayed."

  Files
  - New: pete/index.html and pete/game.js (17 KB).
  - style.css: new farmland color tokens (--leaf*, --sky-*, --arid*, --poison).
    The Catch page styles are now shared by both game pages (.catch, 
    .game-page), plus styles for Pete's wide stage.
  - index.html and catch/index.html: added a "Pete!" nav link and bumped the
    stylesheet cache-buster to v=3.

  The whole site is about 29 KB, far under the 512 KB budget.

  Changes from the plan
  - I darken the scenery at night with a semi-transparent layer instead of
    keeping separate night versions of each sprite. Pete and the hazards are
    drawn on top of it, so they stay bright and easy to see in the dark.
  - I left out the game-over screen flash.
  - The mechanics follow the Dino Game, but all the art is original and
    potato-themed. Nothing is copied from Chrome.

  Worth a look
  - On a phone the play area is only about 140px tall, so Pete is small. That's
    similar to the original game, but the field could be made taller on narrow
    screens.
  - prompts/PETE.md is still untracked, and nothing is committed yet.

     Pete: birds that spawn alongside other hazards, with a fairness guarantee

     Context

     Right now pete/game.js spawns hazards one at a time from a single distance
     countdown (spawn() / s.next). The user wants birds to also appear alongside
     the ground hazards, as pterodactyls do in the Dino Game, so it's harder to
     read when to jump. There is a hard constraint: no impossible situations.
     Any loss should come down to timing alone.

     Two facts make naive overlap unsafe:
     - A high bird directly over a ground hazard is a guaranteed death. Clearing
       the hazard needs a jump, and any jump hits the high bird. Its hitbox
       bottom is 2px above Pete's head (birdHigh top = GROUND-20, box
       [1,3,2,2]).
     - Positions on screen are misleading. Birds fly at 1.25× ground speed and
       beetles at 1.2×, so a bird that spawns behind a weed can catch up with it
       by the time it reaches Pete.

     So fairness has to be judged by when each hazard reaches Pete, not by where
     it is on screen.

     Approach: a fairness check based on arrival times

     All changes are in pete/game.js.

     1. Jump physics, measured once

     At load, simulate the existing jump constants (G, JUMP_V, HOLD_F, MAX_HOLD)
     with a full hold at a small fixed timestep to get:
     - AIR_FULL: total airtime (~0.53s).
     - riseTo(h): time to climb to height h.
     - timeAbove(h): how long Pete stays above h.

     The fairness rules below use these measured values plus margins. If the
     jump is retuned later, the rules adjust automatically instead of relying on
     hard-coded constants.

     2. Each hazard's time window at Pete

     window(o) returns [t0, t1], the seconds from now during which hazard o's
     hitbox overlaps Pete's hitbox horizontally. It's computed as (box.x − 
     petеRight) / (speed·mult) to (box.x + box.w − peteLeft) / (speed·mult),
     using the existing hitbox() and KINDS[k].mult.

     Hazards fall into two groups:
     - Jump hazards (weeds, can, beetle, low bird): Pete must be above need =
       how far the hitbox top sits above GROUND (weedS 12, weedL 18, can 13,
       beetle 7, low bird 11).
     - Stay-down hazards (high bird): Pete must be on the ground (h < 2) for the
       whole window.

     3. isFair(candidate): check the candidate against every hazard already on 
     screen

     - Jump hazard vs. jump hazard is fine if either:
       - One jump clears both: the combined window (earliest t0 to latest t1) ≤
         timeAbove(max need) − SLACK. This covers the existing double-weed
         cluster and a low bird directly over a weed.
       - Pete can land and jump again: the gap between the windows ≥ AIR_FULL − 
         timeAbove(need₂) + REACT.
     - Jump hazard vs. high bird is fine only if the bird's window (padded by
       SLACK) stays outside the jump's envelope, [t0 − riseTo(need) − SLACK, t0 
       − riseTo(need) + AIR_FULL + SLACK]. The envelope is the conservative span
       in which Pete is airborne for that hazard.
     - High bird vs. high bird is always fine, because both just need Pete to
       stay down.
     - Constants: SLACK = 0.08s, which also covers the small speed increase
       while a hazard approaches, and REACT = 0.12s.

     This check is deliberately conservative. It may turn down some combinations
     that skilled play could actually survive, but it never lets an impossible
     one through.

     4. Spawning

     - Main queue: keep spawn() as it is, but run isFair before pushing
       nextKind. If it fails, set s.next = 12 and try again a few px later with
       the same nextKind. The current gap formula and the 60px closing heuristic
       stay, and isFair backs them up.
     - New parallel bird track: a separate distance countdown, s.birdNext. It
       turns on at PARALLEL_AT = 300, with gaps of rand(1.2, 2.5) × (speed·0.6 +
       80) px.
       - When it fires, it picks birdHigh (or birdLow at about 30% once the
         score reaches PARALLEL_LOW_AT = 800) and spawns it at x = W + 4 only if
         isFair passes. Otherwise it retries in 15px.
       - Because both tracks call isFair against everything on screen, neither
         can trap the other.
     - Worst case: if a spawn can't fit, it just waits. Nothing is ever forced.

     Verification

     1. Fairness fuzz test (scratchpad, not committed). Load a debug copy of
        game.js in Node with stubbed DOM/canvas, and expose state, update,
        KINDS. Add an autopilot bot that, every 1/60s step, simulates candidate
        actions forward ~1.5s against the hazards currently on screen (wait;
        jump with a 80/150/300ms hold) and takes one that survives. Run 50+ runs
        with different random seeds to 6000+ points each.
        - Pass: the bot never ends up with no surviving option.
        - Fail: the hazard snapshot gets logged.
        - Also count parallel-bird spawns to confirm the feature actually fires.
     2. Screenshots: headless Chrome of a debug state with a high bird between
        two weeds.
     3. Playtest: play past 300 points in a browser and check that high birds
        sometimes pass while ground hazards are on screen, and that every
        situation is clearable.
     4. node --check pete/game.js. Size stays far under the 512 KB budget.

  How it works (all in pete/game.js)
  - Separate bird track: from 300 points, birds also spawn on their own timer,
    independent of the main hazard queue. High birds start at 300 and low birds
    join in at 800. This makes the pterodactyl-style "do I jump or not?"
    moments.
  - Fairness check: no hazard of any kind spawns unless the game can find a way
    past everything on screen with it added. The check uses Pete's real jump
    physics, the speed ramp, and every tap-to-full-hold jump length.
    - It also builds in 35ms of timing slack, so a valid path never depends on
      frame-perfect input.
    - If the hazard doesn't fit yet, it waits a few pixels and tries again.
      Nothing is ever forced in.
    - It handles a few non-obvious traps: a high bird directly over a weed (an
      automatic death), birds catching up to slower hazards, and full jumps that
      sail over a high bird.
  - No punishment for an old decision: if a hazard appears while Pete is
    mid-jump, it has to be passable whether the player lets go now or keeps
    holding. That decision was made before the hazard showed up.
  - Cost: each check takes about 26µs, roughly once every few frames. game.js is
    now 23 KB.

  A side fix the testing turned up: Pete's jump used to come out slightly
  different on 60Hz and 144Hz screens. Physics now runs in fixed 1/120s steps,
  so the game plays identically on any display. That also keeps the fairness
  check exact.
